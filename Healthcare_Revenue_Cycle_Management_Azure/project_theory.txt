# Healthcare Revenue Cycle Management (RCM) Analytics Platform

## Project Overview

This project focuses on building an end-to-end Healthcare Revenue Cycle Management (RCM) analytics platform using the Azure Data Engineering stack. The objective is to integrate healthcare operational data from multiple sources, standardize it through a Medallion Architecture, and create a business-ready data mart that supports reporting, analytics, and decision-making.

---

# What is Revenue Cycle Management (RCM)?

Revenue Cycle Management (RCM) is the process healthcare organizations use to manage the financial lifecycle of a patient, starting from appointment scheduling and ending when all payments for the services provided have been collected.

The primary goal of RCM is to ensure healthcare providers can continue delivering quality patient care while maintaining financial stability.

---

# Revenue Cycle Process

### 1. Patient Appointment

A patient schedules an appointment with a healthcare provider.

### 2. Patient Registration and Visit

Patient information such as demographics and insurance details is collected. This helps determine who is financially responsible for the healthcare services provided.

### 3. Service Delivery

Healthcare services are provided and documented. Examples include:

* Consultations
* Laboratory tests
* Prescriptions
* Medical procedures
* Therapies

### 4. Billing and Claim Creation

The healthcare provider generates a bill and submits a claim to the insurance company for reimbursement.

### 5. Claim Adjudication

The insurance company reviews the claim and may:

* Approve payment
* Partially approve payment
* Deny payment

### 6. Payment Collection

Payments are received from insurance companies, patients, or both. Outstanding balances are tracked and followed up.

### 7. Revenue Analysis

Organizations monitor financial performance through key metrics and reports to improve revenue collection efficiency.

---

# Focus Area: Accounts Receivable (AR)

Revenue Cycle Management consists of multiple financial functions. This project primarily focuses on Accounts Receivable (AR).

### Accounts Receivable (AR)

Money owed to the healthcare provider for services already delivered.

Examples:

* Insurance reimbursements
* Patient payments
* Co-payments
* Deductibles

### Accounts Payable (AP)

Money paid by healthcare organizations for operational expenses such as salaries and vendor payments.

AP is outside the scope of this project.

---

# Why Accounts Receivable is Important

Healthy Accounts Receivable directly impacts the financial health of healthcare organizations.

Benefits include:

* Improved cash flow
* Faster revenue collection
* Reduced financial risk
* Better operational efficiency

The likelihood of collecting outstanding balances decreases as invoices age.

Typical industry trends:

| Invoice Age          | Collection Probability |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Less than 30 Days    | 93%                    |
| 31 - 60 Days         | 85%                    |
| 61 - 90 Days         | 73%                    |
| Greater than 90 Days | Significantly Lower    |

# Key RCM Metrics

## 1. AR Greater Than 90 Days

Measures the percentage of receivables that remain unpaid for more than 90 days.

Formula:

AR > 90 Days % = (AR Balance > 90 Days / Total AR Balance) × 100

Industry Benchmark:

Less than 10%

---

## 2. Days in Accounts Receivable

Measures how many days it takes on average to collect outstanding receivables.

Formula:

Days in AR = AR Balance / Average Daily Gross Charges

Industry Benchmark:

30 - 45 Days

---

## 3. Net Collection Rate (NCR)

Measures how effectively an organization collects the revenue it is entitled to receive.

Formula:

Net Collection Rate = Payments Received / (Charges - Adjustments)

Industry Benchmark:

Greater than 95%

# Data Engineering Challenge

Healthcare data is often distributed across multiple systems and formats.

Common challenges include:

* Multiple source systems
* Different database schemas
* Data quality issues
* Duplicate records
* Missing values
* External reference datasets
* Monthly file-based data exchanges

The goal is to create a centralized and standardized analytical platform that supports enterprise reporting and KPI tracking.

---

# Data Sources

## 1. Electronic Medical Records (EMR)

Source: Azure SQL Database

Contains operational healthcare data including:

* Patients
* Providers
* Departments
* Encounters
* Transactions

---

## 2. Claims Data

Source: CSV Files

Monthly claim files received from insurance companies containing:

* Claim information
* Payment details
* Adjustments
* Reimbursement status

---

## 3. National Provider Identifier (NPI)

Source: Public API

Provides reference information about healthcare providers including unique NPI identifiers.

---

## 4. ICD Reference Data

Source: Public API

International Classification of Diseases (ICD) codes used to standardize diagnosis information.

Provides:

* Diagnosis Code
* Disease Description
* Disease Classification

# Hospital Merger Use Case

Consider two hospitals, H1 and H2, that have recently merged.

Although both hospitals maintain patient information, their databases may use different schemas and naming conventions.

Example:

Hospital H1:

* patient_id
* first_name
* last_name
* dob

Hospital H2:

* member_id
* fname
* lname
* birth_date

To create unified reporting across both organizations, a Common Data Model (CDM) is implemented.

Key techniques include:

* Schema standardization
* Surrogate key generation
* Data harmonization
* Source system tracking

# Solution Architecture

The project follows a Medallion Architecture pattern.

Landing Zone → Bronze Layer → Silver Layer → Gold Layer

---

## Landing Zone

Stores raw incoming data from all source systems.

Purpose:

* Preserve original data
* Enable auditability
* Support reprocessing

---

## Bronze Layer

Storage Format: Parquet

Purpose:

* Raw ingestion layer
* Source-aligned storage
* Historical retention

Acts as the source of truth.

---

## Silver Layer

Storage Format: Delta Lake

Purpose:

* Data cleansing
* Data standardization
* Deduplication
* Common Data Model implementation
* Surrogate key generation
* SCD Type 2 processing
* Data quality validation

Produces trusted and conformed datasets.

---

## Gold Layer

Storage Format: Delta Lake

Purpose:

* Business-ready analytics layer
* Star schema implementation
* Fact and dimension modeling
* KPI reporting
* Self-service analytics

Used by:

* Power BI dashboards
* Business analysts
* Ad-hoc reporting
* Machine Learning workloads
